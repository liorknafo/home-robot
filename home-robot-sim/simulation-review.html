The robot controls movement

Mapping rotates to observe the room, triangulates nearby points from both cameras, builds free space and obstacles, then selects its own reachable unexplored boundary. It revisits uncertain areas and saves partial progress. A heuristic stopping rule applies only to observed reachable space; it cannot certify unseen rooms.

Go to position recognizes saved 3D landmarks before translating, plans through known free space and corrects its motion from camera observations. It stops travel and searches again if confidence drops. Arrival requires five camera updates within 10 cm of the target estimate, then it switches to Idle.

Autonomous mapping observed 20.6 m² before a deliberate stop to test navigation. From about 1.3 m away, Go to position recognized the saved map, planned and followed its path, then returned to Idle with motor power off. Independent final target error was 9.4 cm. Mapping coverage is partial; full-house completion is not established. Detailed autonomous results.

Reflections and uncertain matches

Depth is accepted only when the two stereo directions agree and the estimated depth error is small enough. Features pass geometric outlier checks; occupancy updates reject reconstructed points below the floor. Temporal tracking and saved landmarks provide additional checks. These safeguards do not guarantee rejection of every reflection.

The polished and matte captures were compared at the same stationary pose. The finish changes the rendered camera pixels, but this is a material check, not proof of robust navigation across all clean floors. Screen-space reflections omit objects outside the camera view. Material comparison.

Using the Jetson

The default ORB tracker is lightweight. An optional SuperPoint + LightGlue backend is included for experiments. In a five-frame desktop CPU comparison, learned matching produced more temporal matches on the difficult frame but cost about 0.8–0.9 seconds per pair, versus about 0.02–0.03 seconds for ORB after warm-up. More matches alone do not prove better localization.

The Jetson has not been benchmarked. Original Nano and Orin boards need different compatible software environments. The current desktop PyTorch installation is not a validated Nano deployment. Benchmark the actual board before selecting a learned model; the live controller stops motion when camera processing falls behind.

Earlier plain-tile experiment

This historical recording used a predefined encoder route and plain 60 cm tiles, before the autonomous modes, terrazzo, white ceiling and surface reflections were added.

The encoder controller reached all its estimated waypoints. The physical route failed: final target error was 83 cm (15 cm test tolerance). The image-only mapper lost tracking on the plain tiled scene. The encoder controller can continue even when the offline camera estimate fails; these are separate results. Route measurements.

The virtual stereo pair has a 62 mm baseline at a height of 150 mm. It produces paired 640 × 360 images with a shared simulation timestamp. The tiled house has plain walls, repeated grout lines, furniture legs and occlusion. This is a substantially harder scene than the earlier calibration room.

84 of 160 pairs were tracked. Tracking was lost at frame 85. The camera-only mapper is not yet reliable on this tiled-house route. Maximum position error before any tracking loss: 9.7 cm; this does not score the untracked remainder.

Earlier easy-scene baseline

The earlier patterned calibration room tracked 66 of 66 pairs on a 40 cm square, with about 2 cm maximum position error. That result only established that the pipeline ran; it does not establish reliable tracking in a normal home. The original recording and measurements are preserved separately.

The vision program matches features between images, obtains their depth from the stereo pair, and estimates camera motion with RANSAC/PnP. Accepted depth points are accumulated into a colored 3D point cloud. This historical cloud is sparse and contains stereo outliers. The newer autonomous controller builds a separate occupancy map from filtered stereo rays. It stops and reports a loss if tracking fails; it does not substitute the simulator position.

The newer controller adds saved-landmark relocalization, occupancy mapping, frontier selection, path planning and live obstacle checks. Global pose-graph optimization, reliable full-house exploration and hardware integration remain unfinished.

Run the robot

  1. Double-click Scripts/launch.command in the delivered folder. It opens the house in Unreal.
  2. Double-click Scripts/start-autonomy.command. On first use it installs the vision libraries into a private environment.
  3. Open Robot controls and choose Start Mapping. The robot scans, chooses reachable frontiers and saves its progress.
  4. Select a pale free-space point on the robot’s map, then choose Go to selected position. The robot first recognizes saved landmarks, plans a path, checks its position while moving and returns to Idle after confirming arrival.
  5. Idle / Stop stops motion and saves partial mapping. Failure to locate itself, a blocked route or a sensor stop is reported without claiming arrival.
  6. In Unreal, use 1 for follow, 2 for robot eye, 3 for house orbit and 4 for top. Drag or use arrows to rotate, and scroll or use + / − to zoom. These controls do not move the stereo sensors.

Run only one simulation and one controller at a time. The autonomous controller owns the motor switch while running. For manual driving, stop that controller first; then use P for power, R to arm, W/S and A/D to move, Space for motor power OFF and C to record stereo. The older drive-house launcher follows a predefined diagnostic route and is separate from the autonomous modes.

Maps are stored under Saved/Maps; recordings are stored in individual Saved/Stereo folders. Ground truth is retained only for independent scoring and is discarded by the robot's sensor adapter. The package includes selected evaluation recordings and saved maps; longer development recordings remain in the local project.

Command example: {"v_m_s":0.1,"w_rad_s":0,"arm":true}. Send velocity heartbeats at 20 Hz; loss for 0.35 seconds disarms the controller. Switch fields are motor_power_off and motor_power_on. Turning power on does not arm. Legacy estop/release_estop input aliases are retained.

Robot data use metres, seconds, +X forward, +Y left, +Z up. Unreal internally uses centimetres and a reflected Y axis. Stereo camera coordinates are X right, Y down, Z forward. The controller can later use a serial Pico adapter and real camera frames; no Pico firmware has been flashed or proven on hardware.

What this simulation can establish

It checks that the code runs, sensor messages have consistent units, the controller reacts to specified faults, and a vision pipeline can estimate movement from a controlled synthetic scene. It cannot establish real motor torque, power integrity, battery condition, camera calibration or floor-sensor reliability.

  • Masses are estimates from CAD and component allowances; chassis inertia is approximated by a box and shifted center of mass.
  • Wheel torque is capped at an assumed 0.10 N·m per motor. Gearmotor reflected inertia is approximated as 0.0005 kg·m² on each wheel axle. Friction and drag are assumed.
  • The caster is an ideal sphere with a bearing constraint. Threshold results are particularly sensitive to contact physics, available torque, weight and caster geometry.
  • Stereo uses an ideal pinhole camera, synthetic 80° horizontal field of view and zero lens distortion. Real rolling shutter, exposure mismatch, USB latency and calibration error are not yet modeled.
  • Encoder counts are quantized, but sensor noise, transparent/black floors, stale range readings, dropouts and electronics faults still need dedicated cases.
  • The 8 × 6 m furnished sample house is a test fixture; it is not a scan of your home. The earlier encoder route visited three rooms. Current autonomous coverage is partial and does not validate every room. The current scene has approximate screen-space floor reflections; off-screen reflected objects, transparent glass, true mirrors and real sensor noise are not represented. Rounded household props have conservative box collision envelopes.

The updated physical design uses a normal motor ON/OFF toggle and organized cable bundles. Wire envelopes clear all three chassis plates in CAD, and the 27 print parts fit the Prusa MK3S bed. Open the revised assembly.
