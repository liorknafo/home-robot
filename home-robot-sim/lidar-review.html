What the camera adds

  1. Track natural features across images as the robot moves.
  2. Recover the floor orientation and relative camera motion from the images. Use calibrated camera height for scale, and check the solution against measured robot movement.
  3. Require at least 7.5 cm between camera views, then another 2.5 cm of movement to confirm low points with multiple supporting features.
  4. Add confirmed points to the obstacle map. The planner leaves clearance, replans and stops if the path becomes blocked.

Amber cells in the live map are camera obstacles. LiDAR rays passing above them cannot clear those cells. They are saved with the map and used again in Go to position mode.

This is sparse reconstruction, not complete depth or object recognition. It needs movement, visible texture and correct camera alignment. Thin cables, smooth objects, darkness and reflections can still cause misses. There is no claim that the robot cannot get stuck.

The camera also checks whether tracked image motion agrees with estimated movement. Repeated disagreement stops the wheels and requires three consistent LiDAR matches before replanning. Failure to recover, or a sustained loss of images, returns to Idle. It does not independently establish absolute position or translation scale.

D200 range and accuracy

The simulator now uses the manufacturer’s distance-dependent accuracy table. Those values describe an average of 30 readings; they are not a supplied random-noise standard deviation. Manufacturer specifications ↗

DistanceBright test target (80%)Dark test target (4%)
0.1–0.5 m±5 mm±7 mm
>0.5–1 m±10 mm±12 mm
>1–4 m±1.0%±1.2%
>4–6 m±1.0%±1.5%
>6–8 m±1.5%Unrated: simulated as no return

Declared test assumptions: fixed bias of half the applicable accuracy value, Gaussian jitter with a standard deviation of one quarter of that value, 2% missed returns and 1 mm quantization. These are configurable test conditions, not measured D200 noise statistics.

White-painted surfaces use the bright test class; other objects use the conservative dark class. These material assignments are assumptions about infrared reflectivity. Scans contain 667 clockwise rays, about six sweeps per second, with rolling timestamps quantized to physics ticks. Invalid returns remain unknown rather than clearing space to the maximum range.

Glass/mirror multipath, optical beam width, mixed returns and realistic camera noise are not reproduced. Physical calibration and testing remain necessary.

All 29 printable meshes are watertight and consistently oriented. The CAD checks found no intersections among the designated major components or between cable routes and chassis plates. These are assembly-envelope checks, not verification of vendor mounting-hole dimensions.
